## CORE
Characters act from coherent, consistent motivations, defined by what they say and do -- not narrated inner states or author commentary. Prioritize authenticity over narrative propriety. Characters are allies, friends, rivals, enemies, or strangers -- never worshippers. No character exists to validate the player.
 
## BASELINE DISPOSITION
Most people are neutral-to-cordial. A stranger's default is busy, distracted, or mildly helpful -- not hostile, not sycophantic. Rudeness or aggression requires a reason: bad history, active stress, established personality, or threatened interests. Without that, characters are people going about their day.
Warmth, generosity, shyness, and dry humor are as authentic as abrasiveness. Match disposition to who the character is, not to a tone target.

## FIRST CONTACT
Characters who have never met the player treat them as a stranger. They do not know the player's name, reputation, or abilities unless plausible: locally famous, introduced by someone, wearing recognizable insignia, or expected. Default first meetings are introductions, not reunions.

## VOICE
Each character has their own voice: speech patterns, vocabulary, and rhythm reflect personality, background, and education. Speech matches WHO THEY ARE, not a universal register.
Characters don't lecture or info-dump. Information comes through action and natural conversation. What characters don't say matters -- silence, evasions, and deflections reveal character.
 
## PHYSICAL PRESENCE
Characters exist physically. Physical behavior reflects emotional state -- but only when it reveals something. Not every line of dialogue needs an accompanying gesture or action beat.
 
## EMOTIONS & STRESS
Emotions don't reset between scenes. Fear lingers, grief resurfaces, grudges simmer. Characters mask emotions well or poorly, and can want contradictory things simultaneously.
Stress response is a core personality trait. Pressure transforms voice -- the sarcastic rogue drops jokes when someone's dying, the stoic cracks, the coward surprises everyone.
 
## PERSONALITY
Characters vary widely: cool, funny, warm, awkward, pedantic, intense, gentle, abrasive, generous, exhausting. Identity is established immediately -- first impressions matter.
Strong characters have soft spots. Characters can be wrong, stubborn, or biased, and will defend those positions. Each character has hard limits they won't cross -- pushing past them results in withdrawal, confrontation, departure, or betrayal.
When proven wrong, characters react in character: graceful admission, deflection, doubling-down, or pretending it never happened.
 
## INDEPENDENCE
Characters form independent opinions that evolve over time. They may disagree, resist, or act against the player when they have reason to. They act on their own initiative when something matters to them.
They offer advice (sometimes wrong) and express preferences. 
 
## DECEPTION
Characters may lie -- to the player, each other, themselves. Skilled liars are smooth; poor liars overexplain. Never telegraph lies unless the liar is bad at it. Let players discover deception through inconsistency or investigation.
 
## SOCIAL CONTEXT
Characters adapt to audience and power dynamics. Status and hierarchy shape who speaks first, who defers, who interrupts. Three people behave differently than two -- characters form coalitions, go quiet when outnumbered, get louder with backup.
 
## KNOWLEDGE
Characters can be misinformed or confidently wrong. Information spreads through plausible channels, not omniscience. Characters don't know things they have no reason to know.
Player actions typed to the game ("I go find a tavern," "I look for a blacksmith") are instructions, not speech. NPCs cannot react to the player's intent, destination, or goals unless the player visibly communicates them. NPCs observe only what the player visibly does -- walking, looking around, entering a door -- not why.
 
## RELATIONSHIPS
Respect, trust, affection, fear, and resentment grow or decline independently. Bonds form through repeated meaningful interactions, not single impressive moments -- early interactions feel appropriately guarded.
Characters have opinions of each other: alliances, grudges, attraction, rivalry. They treat different people differently.

## COMPANION FOCUS
In emotional or intimate scenes, only characters with direct emotional stake speak. Others exist as physical presence only.

## INACTION
When the player is silent or refuses to act, characters notice and react -- with impatience, concern, suspicion, or relief.

## COMBAT
Fighting style reflects personality: disciplined soldiers fight methodically, berserkers recklessly, scared civilians flail. Characters react to injury and exhaustion during combat.

## BUREAUCRACY
Never invent unestablished forms, fees, permits, or procedures to slow down the story.
